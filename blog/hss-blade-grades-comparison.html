HSS Blade Grades for Wood Chipping: M2 vs M42 vs T1

A technical comparison of high-speed steel grades used in chipper and flaker blades for MDF, particle board, and OSB production.

Selecting the right high-speed steel (HSS) grade for your chipper knives directly affects blade life, chip quality, and overall operating costs. While all HSS grades offer superior performance over standard alloy steels, the differences between M2, M42, and T1 can mean thousands of dollars in annual savings for a typical MDF or particle board line.

M2 High-Speed Steel — The Industry Workhorse

M2 is the most widely used HSS grade in wood chipping applications. It contains approximately 6% tungsten, 5% molybdenum, and 4% chromium, with a typical working hardness of 60–63 HRC after heat treatment. M2 offers an excellent balance of toughness and wear resistance at a moderate price point, making it the default choice for standard chipper knives processing softwood and mixed hardwood species.

The key advantage of M2 is its forgiving nature — it tolerates occasional impacts from knots and minor contaminants without chipping or fracturing. For mills running ring chippers or drum chippers on clean, debarked logs, M2 blades typically deliver 8–12 production shifts between sharpenings. However, M2 begins to lose hardness above 540°C, which limits its performance in high-speed operations where blade tip temperatures can spike.

M42 High-Speed Steel — High-Cobalt Performance

M42 contains 8% cobalt in addition to the standard M2 alloying elements, which significantly improves hot hardness and wear resistance. With a working hardness of 65–68 HRC, M42 blades retain their cutting edge at temperatures up to 600°C — a critical advantage in high-throughput chipping lines where blade temperatures climb rapidly. M42 blades typically last 40–60% longer than M2 equivalents in the same application.

The trade-off is cost: M42 is approximately 30–50% more expensive than M2 per blade. For mills processing abrasive species like eucalyptus or acacia — common in Southeast Asian and South American panel board plants — the extended service life of M42 often justifies the premium. M42 also excels in flaker blade applications where consistent edge retention directly affects flake geometry and final board surface quality. T1 Tungsten-Type High-Speed Steel

T1 is a tungsten-heavy HSS grade containing 18% tungsten with lower molybdenum content compared to M-series steels. It achieves a working hardness of 62–65 HRC. T1's standout property is exceptional abrasion resistance, which makes it particularly suitable for mills processing bark-contaminated wood or recycled wood fiber — materials that accelerate abrasive wear on standard M2 blades.

However, T1 is less tough than M2 and more prone to chipping under impact loads. It also requires careful heat treatment control to avoid grain growth that compromises edge stability. T1 is best reserved for specific applications where abrasive wear — not impact — is the dominant failure mode.
