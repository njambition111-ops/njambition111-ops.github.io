Knife Ring Maintenance for Pallmann and Siempelkamp Systems

Equipment-specific guidance on inspection intervals, wear pattern analysis, ring resurfacing, and blade replacement strategies.

Published: 2026-06-17 · 6 min read

The knife ring is the beating heart of any ring chipper. Its condition directly determines chip quality, energy consumption, and blade life. Pallmann and Siempelkamp systems — the two dominant ring chipper platforms in global MDF production — share many maintenance principles but have critical differences that maintenance teams must understand.

Inspection Frequency: What the Manuals Don't Tell You

OEM manuals typically recommend knife ring inspection every 500-800 operating hours. In practice, mills processing hardwood or mixed species should inspect every 300-400 hours. Early-stage wear is subtle — a 0.1 mm groove in the ring surface may seem insignificant, but it creates a ledge that concentrates stress on the blade edge, accelerating chipping and reducing blade life by up to 40%.

Use a bore gauge or CMM to measure ring bore diameter at three axial positions during each inspection. Record measurements in a trend log. A diameter increase of more than 0.15 mm from baseline indicates that ring wear has entered the acceleration phase and resurfacing should be scheduled.

Pallmann Ring Chippers: Specific Considerations

Pallmann PHT and PHTR series knife rings use a segmented design — individual blade holders bolt onto the ring body. The primary wear point on these systems is the blade holder contact face, not the ring body itself. When holder faces wear unevenly, blades sit at inconsistent angles, producing erratic chip geometry.

During inspection, check each holder face with a straightedge and feeler gauge. If any holder shows more than 0.05 mm of deviation, the entire set of holders should be surface-ground as a matched set. Never replace individual holders — even new holders from the same batch may have slight dimensional variation.

For Pallmann-compatible chipper knives, we manufacture to exact OEM dimensions with tolerance held to ±0.02 mm on mounting hole positions.

Siempelkamp Ring Chippers: Different Design, Different Care

Siempelkamp uses a monolithic knife ring design where blades mount directly into machined slots in the ring body. This simplifies blade alignment but makes ring condition even more critical — there are no replaceable holders to absorb wear. Any damage to the slot walls transfers directly to blade positioning.

The most common Siempelkamp ring failure mode is slot wall deformation caused by single-blade failure events. When one blade fractures, the impact can deform adjacent slot walls by 0.1-0.3 mm. If this happens, the ring must be removed and the affected slots machined back to specification — continued operation with deformed slots will destroy new blades within hours.

Blade Replacement Strategy: Full Set vs. Rotational

For both Pallmann and Siempelkamp systems, we recommend full-set blade replacement rather than rotational replacement. Rotating individual blades (replacing only the most worn) creates an unbalanced ring where some blades cut efficiently while others produce oversized chips. The resulting chip-size variation disrupts mat formation in the forming line.

Keep one complete spare blade set on hand at all times. When blade edge wear reaches 0.3 mm from the original edge profile, schedule a full change-out during the next planned downtime window. The removed set can be re-sharpened and stored as the next spare.

Maintenance Checklist Summary

  • Every 300-400 hours: Visual inspection of ring surface and bore measurement (3 positions).
  • Every blade change: Check holder faces (Pallmann) or slot walls (Siempelkamp) for wear.
  • Ring runout check: Monthly, using dial indicator. TIR max 0.03 mm.
  • Ring resurfacing: When bore diameter exceeds baseline by >0.15 mm.
  • Blade replacement: Always full-set. Edge wear limit: 0.3 mm from original profile.
