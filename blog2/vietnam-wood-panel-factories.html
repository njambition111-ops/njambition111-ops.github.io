Vietnam's Wood Panel Factory Expansion: Blade Supply Chain Guide

Home › Blog › Vietnam Panel Industry

Published: June 21, 2026 | By PanelKnife

Vietnam has emerged as one of the fastest-growing wood panel manufacturing hubs in the world. In just five years, the country's MDF production capacity has more than tripled, rising from approximately 1.2 million cubic meters in 2021 to an estimated 3.8 million cubic meters in 2026. This expansion has fundamentally changed the regional blade supply landscape. For plant managers and procurement teams operating in Vietnam, understanding the local production ecosystem is essential to maintaining reliable blade supply.

Major Production Zones

Vietnam's wood panel industry clusters in two primary zones. Binh Duong province, located just north of Ho Chi Minh City, hosts the densest concentration of MDF and particleboard plants in the country. Major producers here include Kim Tin MDF (capacity: 400,000 m³/year), VRG Dongwha (a Vietnam-Korea joint venture, capacity: 300,000 m³/year), and An Cuong Wood Working. The Binh Duong cluster benefits from proximity to the Thi Vai and Cai Mep deep-water ports, which handle both imported raw materials and finished panel exports.

Dong Nai province, east of Ho Chi Minh City, is the second major hub. This zone is home to less dense but larger individual plants, including the Dongwha Green MDF facility and several Taiwanese-invested particleboard lines. Together, Binh Duong and Dong Nai account for roughly 70% of Vietnam's total wood panel output. A smaller but growing cluster exists around Quang Tri in central Vietnam, driven by acacia plantation availability from the adjacent highlands.

Machine Brands in Operation

The Vietnamese panel industry runs a diverse mix of equipment. German brands dominate the continuous press lines: Siempelkamp and Dieffenbacher presses are installed at most major MDF plants. For chipping and flaking, Pallmann and Maier (both German) are the most common, followed by Klöckner drum chippers at older facilities. Chinese equipment from Yalian and Sufoma has gained significant market share in new mid-capacity plants over the past three years, particularly in particleboard lines under 100,000 m³/year.

This mixed machine park creates a complex blade supply requirement. A single large plant may need chipper knives for a Klöckner UHM-600, flaker blades for a Pallmann PZKR ring flaker, and counter-knives for a Maier chipping line — all in different dimensions and steel grades.

Blade Supply Chain Requirements

Vietnam's tropical climate and raw material mix impose specific demands on blade performance. Much of the wood feedstock comes from acacia and rubberwood plantations, both of which contain high silica content that accelerates blade wear. Rubberwood in particular, with its latex residues, can be mildly corrosive to blade edges during extended production runs. As a result, Vietnamese plants typically consume blades at 15–25% higher rates than equivalent plants in temperate regions processing pine or spruce.

This means blade suppliers must offer fast turnaround on standard sizes, hold local or regional buffer stock, and provide technical support for optimizing blade life under local conditions. Delivery lead times exceeding two weeks are generally unacceptable for plants running 24/7 production. Many Vietnamese plants keep 3–4 complete blade sets in rotation: one in the machine, one being sharpened, one in transit, and one as emergency backup.
