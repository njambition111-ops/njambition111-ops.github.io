Blade Requirements for Dieffenbacher and Siempelkamp Production Lines

Home › Blog › Dieffenbacher & Siempelkamp Blades

Published: June 21, 2026 | By PanelKnife

Dieffenbacher and Siempelkamp are the two dominant names in European wood-based panel press technology, each with a comprehensive portfolio spanning complete MDF, particle board, and OSB production lines. While the continuous press is their best-known product, both manufacturers supply full front-end equipment including chippers and flakers — each with specific blade requirements that production managers must understand for efficient operations and cost-effective procurement.

Dieffenbacher Production Line Configuration

A typical Dieffenbacher MDF or particle board line begins with a drum chipper for primary size reduction, followed by a screening system that separates oversize material for re-chipping. Accepted chips proceed to knife ring flakers — Dieffenbacher often integrates Pallmann PHT or PZKR series flakers in their front-end packages, as the two companies have a long-standing engineering partnership. The chipper knives for Dieffenbacher drum chippers typically require HSS or tungsten carbide edges with specific mounting patterns and knife dimensions that vary by chipper model and throughput capacity.

Flaker blades in Dieffenbacher lines must produce consistent surface layer flakes (typically 0.15-0.35mm thickness) and core layer flakes (0.35-0.70mm thickness). Blade edge angle, sharpness, and material grade directly determine whether the flake thickness distribution falls within these tight specification windows. PanelKnife manufactures precision flaker blades engineered to maintain flake thickness tolerances throughout the full service interval between sharpenings.

Siempelkamp Production Line Configuration

Siempelkamp front-end systems frequently feature a combination of disc chippers for initial wood size reduction and knife ring flakers for flake production. Siempelkamp's engineering approach emphasizes high-throughput continuous operation, and their chipper specifications demand blades with excellent edge retention under sustained production loads. The company's ContiRoll press technology is often paired with front-end equipment optimized for the specific wood species and desired board characteristics in each installation.

Siempelkamp knife ring flakers — typically manufactured in-house or sourced from strategic partners — require blades with precise dimensional accuracy and consistent hardness across the entire blade body. Knife ring diameters vary by production capacity, with corresponding blade count ranging from 36 to 54 knives per ring. Our custom blade manufacturing service supports Siempelkamp flaker rings of all sizes, producing blades that match or exceed OEM dimensional specifications.

Comparable Blade Specifications

While Dieffenbacher and Siempelkamp each have proprietary knife geometries and mounting systems, the underlying blade requirements share common ground:

  • Material grades: Both lines benefit from A8B for impact-prone applications and CH5 or D2 for high-wear environments. Tool steel selection should account for the wood species and contamination risk profile of each mill
  • Hardness range: HRC 58-62 is the standard specification window for both manufacturers, with tighter upper-limit control for carbide grades
  • Edge geometry: Chipper knife bevel angles typically range from 28 to 35 degrees, while flaker blade angles range from 26 to 32 degrees depending on surface vs core layer application
  • Dimensional tolerance: Both OEMs require knife body dimensions within ±0.05mm and mounting hole positions within ±0.03mm to ensure proper clamping and balanced rotation
  • Surface finish: Mounting surfaces must achieve Ra 0.8µm or better to prevent stress concentrations and ensure uniform clamping force distribution

OEM vs Aftermarket Blades: The Real Comparison

OEM blades from Dieffenbacher and Siempelkamp carry the engineering authority of the machine manufacturer but typically command premium pricing — often 30-50% higher than equivalent aftermarket blades. The key question for production managers is whether the premium translates to measurable performance advantages.
