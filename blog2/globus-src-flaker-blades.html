Globus SRC Knife Ring Flaker Blades: Specifications and Compatibility

Home › Blog › Globus SRC Flaker Blades

Published: June 21, 2026 | By PanelKnife

The Globus SRC knife ring flaker is a workhorse in particle board and MDF production lines worldwide, recognized for its high throughput and consistent flake geometry. Central to its performance is the knife ring assembly — a precision-engineered system that can accommodate up to 54 individual knives arranged around a rotating ring with a wobble disc distribution mechanism. Understanding the specifications, materials, and maintenance requirements of Globus SRC flaker blades is essential for production managers seeking reliable replacement sources and optimal flake quality.

The Globus SRC Flaker Architecture

Globus SRC flakers employ a knife ring design where blades are mounted on the inner circumference of a rotating ring. Raw material is fed into the center and distributed outward by a wobble disc — a rotating inclined plate that throws material against the knife edges using centrifugal force. This distribution system ensures uniform loading across all knives, which is critical for consistent flake thickness and minimizing premature wear on individual blade positions.

The knife ring can be configured with up to 54 knives depending on the SRC model variant and desired throughput capacity. Each blade is held in place by a clamping system that must maintain precise radial positioning — even a fraction of a millimeter deviation at one knife position creates uneven flake thickness across the ring, affecting downstream board density distribution.

Blade Dimensions and Configurations

Globus SRC flaker blades are manufactured to exact dimensional tolerances to ensure proper fitment and cutting performance. Typical blade dimensions vary by SRC model series, with common lengths ranging from 180mm to 360mm, widths from 45mm to 80mm, and thicknesses from 6mm to 12mm. Each blade features precision-ground mounting surfaces and cutting edges ground to a specific angle — typically between 28 and 35 degrees depending on the wood species being processed and whether the blade serves surface layer or core layer production.

Blade mounting holes are positioned to sub-millimeter accuracy, as improper hole placement leads to clamping stress concentrations and potential blade fracture during high-speed operation. PanelKnife manufactures Globus SRC replacement blades using CNC machining centers that maintain hole position tolerances within ±0.05mm of OEM specifications.

Material Selection: CH5 and A8B

Two tool steel grades dominate Globus SRC blade applications: CH5 and A8B. CH5 is a high-carbon, high-chromium cold-work steel with excellent wear resistance, making it the preferred choice for high-volume particle board production where abrasive wood species dominate the feedstock. Its chromium carbide structure maintains edge sharpness through extended production runs, reducing blade change frequency. A8B, a modified air-hardening steel, offers a balanced combination of toughness and wear resistance — ideal for mills processing mixed species or encountering occasional contamination in raw material.

The material choice directly impacts the total cost per cubic meter of production. While CH5 blades typically command a higher purchase price, their extended service life in abrasive conditions often yields a lower cost per production unit compared to standard tool steels. For mills running clean softwood feedstock, A8B provides an excellent balance of performance and procurement cost.

Wobble Disc and Feed Distribution

The wobble disc system in Globus SRC flakers is both an engineering advantage and a maintenance consideration. When functioning correctly, the wobble disc distributes material evenly across all 54 knife positions, preventing localized wear and ensuring uniform flake output. However, a worn or misaligned wobble disc creates feed concentration on a subset of knives, causing those blades to dull rapidly while others remain relatively sharp. Production managers should inspect wobble disc condition during every knife change and replace the disc when wear exceeds OEM tolerance limits — typically 3-5mm of surface wear before distribution uniformity degrades measurably.
