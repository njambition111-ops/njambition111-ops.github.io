OSB Stranding Blade Specifications: Complete Technical Reference

Home › Blog › OSB Stranding Blade Specs

Published: 2026-06-21 | By PanelKnife

Oriented Strand Board (OSB) production places unique demands on cutting tools. Unlike chipper knives that reduce logs to uniform chips, OSB stranding blades must produce precisely dimensioned strands — long, thin wood wafers that will be oriented, blended with resin, and pressed into structural panels. The blade specifications that work for chip production will not produce acceptable OSB strands. This technical reference covers every critical specification.

OSB Strand Dimension Requirements

OSB panel performance depends on strand geometry. Industry standards define the following target dimensions for face and core layer strands:

  • Strand length: 75-150 mm for face layers, 50-75 mm for core layers. Longer face strands improve panel bending strength and stiffness.
  • Strand width: 15-25 mm. Narrower strands improve surface smoothness; wider strands increase throughput.
  • Strand thickness: 0.5-0.8 mm, with a tolerance of +/- 0.1 mm. Thickness consistency is critical — variation beyond this range causes uneven resin absorption and pressing defects.

Achieving these dimensions consistently requires blades with precisely controlled edge geometry and wear characteristics that hold that geometry throughout the service interval.

Blade Material: Why Toughness Matters More Than Hardness

A common mistake in OSB blade specification is over-prioritizing hardness. While chipper knives benefit from high hardness (60-65 HRC) for wear resistance, OSB stranding blades face a different failure mode: micro-chipping along the cutting edge. The stranding process involves cutting across wood grain at high speed with intermittent contact — each log entering the strander creates an impact load on the blade edge.

When a blade is too hard and brittle, these repeated impacts cause the cutting edge to chip, producing strands with ragged edges and inconsistent thickness. The result is lower panel quality and more frequent blade changes. The preferred material prioritizes toughness — the ability to absorb impact energy without fracturing — over maximum hardness.

A8 Tool Steel: The Industry Standard

A8 tool steel has emerged as the preferred material for OSB stranding blades. With approximately 5% chromium, 1.1% molybdenum, and 1.25% tungsten, A8 delivers an optimal balance of properties:

  • Hardness: 56-60 HRC after heat treatment — lower than M2 or D2, but sufficient for wood cutting with proper edge maintenance.
  • Toughness: Significantly higher than D2 or M2. A8's impact resistance means it can withstand the repeated shock of log entry without micro-chipping.
  • Air-hardening: Minimal distortion during heat treatment, preserving dimensional accuracy across the blade length.
  • Grindability: A8 sharpens well on standard tool grinding equipment with aluminum oxide wheels, keeping maintenance costs manageable.

Some high-volume mills have adopted powder metallurgy (PM) grades such as A11 or CPM-10V for extended edge life, but A8 remains the cost-effective standard for the vast majority of OSB production lines. Cutting Angle and Geometry

The recommended cutting angle (clearance angle relative to the cutting plane) for OSB stranding blades is 35-38°. This range represents a careful compromise: angles below 35° produce cleaner strand edges but accelerate wear and increase chipping risk, while angles above 38° increase cutting force and power consumption. The rake angle should be maintained at 2-3° positive for softwood species (pine, spruce) and 0-1° for hardwood feedstocks.
