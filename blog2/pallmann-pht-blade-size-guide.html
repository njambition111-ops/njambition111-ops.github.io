Pallmann PHT Series Chipper Knife Size Guide: Complete Reference

Home › Blog › Pallmann PHT Blade Sizes

Published: June 21, 2026 | By PanelKnife

The Pallmann PHT series drum chipper is one of the most widely deployed industrial chipping platforms in the global wood processing industry, found in pulp mills, OSB plants, and pellet facilities across six continents. Despite its ubiquity, blade dimensions vary significantly across PHT models — and ordering the wrong size is a costly mistake that can idle a production line for days. This reference guide consolidates blade specifications for every common PHT model, covering dimensions, steel grade options, and mounting configurations.

PHT Model Range and Blade Dimensions

Pallmann PHT drum chippers are designated by rotor diameter and width: a PHT 500x1250 has a 500 mm diameter rotor with 1250 mm working width. Blade dimensions scale with rotor size, and each model typically accepts one or two standard knife sizes depending on the knife holder configuration.

Note that blade width (the dimension from cutting edge to rear edge) is critical for proper knife holder clamping. Running blades that have been ground below minimum width specification risks knife ejection — a catastrophic failure mode. Always verify minimum width tolerance with your machine manual before regrinding.

CH5 vs CH8 Steel Grades

Pallmann specifies two primary steel grades for PHT chipper knives: CH5 and CH8. These are Pallmann's proprietary designations that map approximately to industry-standard tool steel grades.

CH5 is a medium-alloy tool steel analogous to A8/A8B, with approximately 5% chromium content. It offers excellent toughness and is specified for PHT chippers processing hardwood, frozen wood, and log-yard material where impact resistance is paramount. CH5 knives are less sensitive to grinding-induced overheating and are generally more forgiving in less-controlled sharpening environments.

CH8 is a high-alloy grade with approximately 8% chromium content, positioned between A8B and D2 in the wear-resistance spectrum. It provides measurably longer edge life than CH5 in abrasive softwood and clean furnish applications, while retaining adequate toughness for most production scenarios. CH8 is the default specification for PHT chippers in high-volume softwood pulp mills where knife changes every 4-6 hours on CH5 can be extended to 6-8 hours with CH8, reducing annual knife consumption by 25-30%.

For a deeper understanding of how these steel grades compare to industry-standard A8, A8B, and D2, see our complete steel selection guide.

Mounting Hole Patterns

PHT chipper knives use countersunk mounting holes arranged in a linear pattern along the blade length. Hole spacing is standardized within each model series but varies between rotor diameters. Standard specifications include:

  • Hole diameter: 14 mm, 16 mm, or 18 mm depending on rotor size
  • Hole spacing: Typically 100-150 mm center-to-center
  • Countersink angle: 90 degrees to match standard DIN 7991 flat-head cap screws
  • Number of holes: 6-14 per blade depending on blade length

Always confirm the hole pattern from your existing blades or machine documentation before ordering. Even within the same PHT model family, machines manufactured in different years or for different regional markets may have subtle variations in bolt-hole centers.

Ordering Guide

When ordering replacement chipper knives for a Pallmann PHT drum chipper, have the following information ready:

  1. PHT model number (from the machine data plate)
  2. Blade dimensions: Length x Width x Thickness (from existing blade measurement, not manual)
  3. Steel grade preference: CH5 or CH8 (or A8/D2 equivalents)
  4. Hole count and center-to-center spacing
  5. Quantity required: Full set plus recommended spares for rotation
