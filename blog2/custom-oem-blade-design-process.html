From Drawing to Delivery: The Custom OEM Blade Manufacturing Process

Home › Blog › Custom OEM Blade Process

Published: June 21, 2026 | By PanelKnife

Custom OEM blade manufacturing is a precision engineering discipline that transforms a technical drawing or a worn sample blade into a production-ready cutting tool. At Nanjing Ambixin Machine Tool, every custom blade order follows a disciplined seven-stage manufacturing process designed to ensure exact dimensional conformity, consistent material properties, and reliable performance in demanding production environments. Here is how a custom blade moves from concept to delivery.

Stage 1: Technical Drawing Review and CAD Processing

The process begins when the customer submits a technical drawing — typically in DWG, DXF, STEP, or PDF format — or ships a physical sample blade for reverse engineering. Our engineering team reviews the drawing for completeness: overall dimensions, mounting hole positions and diameters, edge angle specification, surface finish requirements, and material grade callout. Any missing or ambiguous specifications are clarified with the customer before the order proceeds to manufacturing.

If a physical sample is provided, our metrology lab performs a full geometric inspection using coordinate measuring equipment, generating a CAD model that captures every dimension to micron-level accuracy. This reverse-engineering capability is particularly valuable for mills operating older equipment where original drawings may no longer be available. The finalized CAD file becomes the single source of truth for all subsequent manufacturing operations.

Stage 2: Material Selection and Procurement

Material selection is application-driven. For chipper knives processing hardwood species, A8 tool steel provides maximum impact toughness. For abrasive softwood or bamboo applications, D2 or CH5 offers superior wear resistance. For flaker blades, A8B balances toughness and edge retention. Our A8 vs D2 steel comparison guide provides detailed selection criteria by wood species and production environment.

Raw material is sourced from certified steel mills with full material certification documentation — chemical composition, heat number traceability, and ultrasonic inspection reports for internal defects. Material is received in annealed condition, inspected for dimensional conformity and surface quality, and assigned a unique batch tracking number that follows the blades through every subsequent manufacturing step.

Stage 3: CNC Machining

Blade blanks are machined on multi-axis CNC machining centers programmed directly from the approved CAD model. This stage produces the overall blade profile, mounting holes, counterbores, and any special features such as chip breaker grooves or coolant channels. Hole position accuracy is maintained within ±0.05mm — a tolerance that is critical because improper hole placement creates uneven clamping forces that can lead to blade cracking during operation.

After machining, blades receive a stress-relief treatment to eliminate residual stresses introduced during material removal. This intermediate heat treatment step prevents dimensional distortion during the subsequent hardening process and is a quality step that distinguishes professional blade manufacturers from lower-tier suppliers.

Stage 4: Vacuum Heat Treatment

Heat treatment is where the blade's metallurgical properties are established. PanelKnife uses vacuum furnaces — not atmosphere or salt bath furnaces — because vacuum processing eliminates surface decarburization and oxidation, producing a clean, scale-free surface that requires less post-treatment grinding. Each blade batch is heated to the austenitizing temperature specified for its steel grade (typically 980-1050°C for A8 family steels, 1000-1040°C for D2), held for a precisely timed soak period, and quenched under controlled cooling rates.

Vacuum furnace temperature uniformity is critical: a variation of even 5°C across the load can produce hardness inconsistencies between blades in the same batch. Our furnaces maintain temperature uniformity within ±3°C, and every cycle is documented with a time-temperature recorder chart that becomes part of the batch quality record.

Stage 5: Double Tempering

After quenching, blades are too brittle for service — hardness may reach HRC 64-66 but toughness is minimal. Tempering reduces hardness to the target range while dramatically improving toughness. PanelKnife performs double tempering: the blades are heated to tempering temperature (typically 520-560°C for secondary hardening steels), held, cooled to room temperature, and then tempered a second time. The double temper cycle ensures complete transformation of retained austenite and provides superior dimensional stability compared to single-temper processes.

Post-tempering hardness is verified on every blade using a calibrated Rockwell hardness tester. Target ranges — typically HRC 58-62 for chipper and flaker blades — are documented per blade. Blades outside the specification window are rejected and do not proceed to grinding.

Stage 6: Precision Grinding to ±0.02mm

The cutting edge and mounting surfaces are precision-ground on CNC grinding machines equipped with automatic wheel dressing and in-process dimensional measurement. The grinding process establishes three critical parameters simultaneously:

  • Edge angle tolerance: The cutting bevel is ground to the specified angle with a tolerance of ±0.5 degrees, verified by optical comparator measurement
  • Surface finish: Mounting surfaces achieve Ra 0.8µm or better to ensure uniform clamping force distribution and prevent stress concentration points
  • Overall dimensional tolerance: Critical dimensions — blade length, width, and thickness — are held within ±0.02mm of nominal, measured at multiple points per blade

Cryogenic treatment may be applied between tempering and final grinding for D2 and CH5 grades, converting additional retained austenite to martensite and improving both hardness uniformity and dimensional stability. This optional step is recommended for blades operating in high-wear environments where maximum edge retention is required.

Stage 7: QC Inspection and Export Packaging

Every blade undergoes individual dimensional inspection against the CAD specification before it is cleared for packaging. Key dimensions are measured and recorded. A batch sampling plan covers hardness testing, with every blade in the batch individually hardness-tested for critical applications. Surface quality is visually inspected under magnification for grinding burns, micro-cracks, or edge defects.

Approved blades are coated with a corrosion-inhibiting oil, individually wrapped in VCI (volatile corrosion inhibitor) paper, and packed in reinforced export-grade cartons or plywood cases depending on order volume and destination. Each shipment includes material certification, heat treatment charts, and dimensional inspection reports — the complete quality documentation package that professional buyers expect from a qualified custom blade manufacturer.
